# Everyone Knows What AI Is. Almost No One Knows HOW.

1. In 1949, Oxford philosopher Gilbert Ryle split all human knowledge into two kinds: *knowing that* and *knowing how*.
2. Knowing *that* is facts. Knowing *how* is skill — you can know every rule of cricket and still not survive one over from Bumrah.
3. In 1903, Samuel Langley — head of the Smithsonian, backed by $50,000 of US Army money — launched his flying machine. It collapsed straight into the Potomac River.
4. Nine days later, two bicycle mechanics with no college degrees flew the world's first airplane. The Wright brothers didn't *know more*. They knew **HOW**.
5. AI is that story, again. Knowing *that* AI exists is now free — every student has ChatGPT open in the next tab.
6. And yet, 73% of Indian employers plan to hire freshers, while nearly 3 in 10 young graduates can't find work.
7. The gap isn't jobs. It isn't degrees. It's **proof** — of what you can actually build.
8. Worse, AI can quietly steal your know-how: in an MIT study, 83% of students who wrote with ChatGPT couldn't quote a single line of the essay they had just "written."
9. When physicist Richard Feynman died, one sentence was left on his blackboard: *"What I cannot create, I do not understand."*
10. That sentence is our philosophy. Wisdom is what you know. Being **wise** is what you do with it.
11. That's why we are **House of Wise (HOW)** — India's AI-ready student movement. No passive learning. No empty certificates. Only capability, proof, and community.
12. Students move through a 7-layer journey from curiosity to capability — and build real things: an AI interview coach, a resume screener, a campus helpdesk bot.
13. Every project, challenge and presentation adds to their **HOW Score** — a 1,000-point measure of AI readiness, from *AI Explorer* to *HOW Visionary*.
14. Inside the **HOW Guild**, they ship in 48-hour build sprints, learn from mentors, and grow alongside people who make things.
15. Everyone knows *what* AI is. Welcome to the House where you learn **HOW**.
